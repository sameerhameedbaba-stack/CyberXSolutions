Our story

It started with a security team drowning in alerts.

CyberXSolutions started with an unglamorous observation: most companies were not short of data, talent or ambition. They were short of systems that could act.

  1. 2019

    A security team drowning in alerts

    Our first engagement was a security operations centre reviewing 40,000 alerts a month by hand. We did not sell them a model. We rebuilt their triage path, automated the 92% that were noise, and gave three analysts their evenings back. That became the template.

  2. 2021

    From scripts to systems

    Automation projects kept dying the same way — a clever script, one edge case, and a workflow nobody trusted again. So we started building automation the way we build software: versioned, tested, observable, and reversible.

  3. 2023

    Agents that could be held accountable

    When language models became capable enough to plan and act, most of the industry shipped chat. We built the boring parts instead — permissions, evaluation harnesses, cost ceilings, audit history — so an agent could be given real authority without becoming a liability.

  4. Today

    Systems that run the work

    We now design, build and operate the AI, automation, cloud and security systems that keep our clients running. Same discipline, larger surface area, and a bar that has only moved up.

How we think

Three philosophies we actually argue about internally.

Innovation philosophy

We do not chase releases. We adopt a technology when it survives a hostile evaluation against the thing it claims to replace, and we retire our own work the moment something better exists. Novelty is not a deliverable.

  • Every new capability enters through a benchmark, not a blog post
  • Prototypes are timeboxed and allowed to fail loudly
  • We publish what we learn, including what did not work

Security philosophy

Assume compromise. Every system we build is designed so that a stolen credential, a bad prompt or a rogue integration produces a contained incident rather than a headline.

  • Least privilege by default, with time-bound and revocable access
  • Every automated action is attributable to an identity and reversible
  • Threat modelling happens at design review, not before launch

Engineering philosophy

Boring, observable systems beat clever, opaque ones. We optimise for the engineer who inherits this in three years — which is often our own client.

  • Typed end to end, tested where it matters, documented as it ships
  • Infrastructure as code, with environments that can be rebuilt from scratch
  • Instrumentation before optimisation — never guess where the cost is

Quality standards

What “done” means here — written down.

These are the criteria every deliverable is checked against before it leaves. They are not aspirational; a build that misses one does not ship.

Definition of done

Tested, documented, instrumented, deployed through a repeatable pipeline, and handed over with a runbook.

Review discipline

Every change is peer-reviewed. Security-sensitive changes get a second reviewer with an explicit threat lens.

Measured baselines

We record the before state — cost, cycle time, error rate — so improvement is a fact rather than an impression.

Accessibility floor

WCAG 2.2 AA is the minimum on everything we ship with a user interface, verified with real assistive technology.

Performance budget

Interfaces are held to Core Web Vitals budgets in CI. Regressions fail the build rather than the launch.

Operational readiness

Alerts, dashboards, on-call runbooks and rollback procedures exist before the first user does.
